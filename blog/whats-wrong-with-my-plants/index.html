What’s Wrong with my Plants? Part 1

Six elements matter most for healthy growth: nitrogen, potassium, phosphorus, magnesium, calcium and iron. Part one covers the first three.

Nitrogen builds the green parts of a plant and matters most in the growing season. Too little brings slow, weak growth, yellowing and browning leaves, and stems that turn woody early. Too much gives oversized plants with dark, dense foliage, late flowers, and greater vulnerability to cold and fungal disease.

Potassium regulates water balance in plant cells and supports drought tolerance, disease resistance and frost hardiness. A shortage causes weak growth, late and pale flowers, yellow edges on older leaves that later brown and curl, a bluish-green tint, wilting and poor branching. An excess usually means salty soil, which makes plants wilt and dry out.

Phosphorus is vital for flowering and seed. A shortage means late, poor flowering, weak fruiting and bluish-green leaves with purple spots, and lower leaves may turn orange.
