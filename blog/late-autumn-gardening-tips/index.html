Late Autumn Gardening Tips

Preparing the garden for winter is worthwhile. Lifting some perennials and storing them over winter can mean bigger, brighter flowers the following spring.

To lift plants: cut them down shortly after the first frost; lift carefully with a fork to protect the roots; shake off excess soil and let them dry; pack roots or tubers in open wooden trays with nearly dry potting compost or sand; store the trays somewhere dry, cool and frost-free, such as a greenhouse or shed; and check regularly for rot, removing anything unhealthy.

Chrysanthemum roots may need a heated greenhouse and watering in mid to late winter to restart growth. Dahlia tubers should stay cool and dry all winter.

Suitable plants for lifting include dahlias, cannas, tuberous begonias, gladioli, pelargoniums and chrysanthemums. For large, sensitive plants that cannot be lifted, such as artichokes and agapanthus, cover the bed with a thick mulch before winter.
