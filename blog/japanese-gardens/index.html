Japanese Gardens

Japanese gardens reflect a deep love of landscape. Their designs draw on the country’s native greenery and on older Chinese garden traditions, while staying close to everyday life. The earliest records date from the Suiko period (552–645), and eight distinct styles are now recognised.

Most share a handful of features: natural rock and stone paving, some of it practical, such as a basin for washing; ponds with jets or fountains; islands, cascades, bridges and hills; and plenty of greenery.

Evergreens dominate and pine is the most prized, standing for longevity. Maples are the favourite deciduous tree, alongside cherry, magnolia, camellia, wisteria and hydrangea, with irises, lilies, asters and anemones beneath.

A central principle is nature in miniature: trees and shrubs are pruned with great care so that they look natural and picturesque, as they would in the wild.
